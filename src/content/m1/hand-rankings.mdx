## Nine categories, ordered by rarity

A poker hand is exactly five cards. Every five-card hand falls into one of nine categories, and the categories are ranked by how hard they are to get. The rarer the hand, the more it beats.

## Ties and kickers

Within a category, hands are compared by the ranks of their cards. The rule is always the same: compare the most important cards first, and move to the next only if they are equal.

- **Pair against pair.** The higher pair wins. If the pairs match, compare the three side cards from highest to lowest. Those side cards are called **kickers**.
- **Two pair.** Compare the higher pair, then the lower pair, then the one kicker.
- **Three of a kind.** Compare the three, then the two kickers.
- **Straight.** Only the top card matters.
- **Flush.** Compare all five cards from the top down.
- **Full house.** Compare the three, then the pair.

Suits never break ties. The ace of spades is worth exactly as much as the ace of clubs. If two hands have the same five ranks, they split the pot.

## The ace goes both ways

The ace is the highest card, but it can also sit below the two to complete the straight A-2-3-4-5. That straight is called the wheel and it is the lowest straight there is: it loses to 2-3-4-5-6. The ace cannot be in the middle. Q-K-A-2-3 is not a straight.

## Best five of seven

In Hold'em you will have seven cards to work with: two of your own and five shared. Your hand is the best five-card hand you can make from them. The other two cards do not exist for any purpose.

<Remember>

- Order, best to worst: straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair, high card
- The order is the order of rarity. Rarer beats more common
- A hand is exactly five cards. Kickers break ties, suits never do
- The ace is high, and also low in A-2-3-4-5 only
- With seven cards, only the best five count

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Thinking a straight beats a flush. It is the other way round
- Counting a sixth card as a tiebreaker. Only five cards play
- Ranking suits. In poker all four suits are equal
- Ignoring the kicker when starting a hand. Top pair with a weak kicker often loses to top pair with a better one

</Mistakes>
