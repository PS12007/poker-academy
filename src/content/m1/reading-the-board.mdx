## Your hand is only as good as the board lets it be

A pair of aces is the best starting hand. On a board of 9-8-7-6 with three hearts, it is close to worthless. Nothing about the aces changed. The board changed what other players can have.

Reading the board means answering three questions every time new cards arrive:

1. What is the best possible hand?
2. What draws are possible?
3. How does my hand rank among everything that is possible?

## The nuts

The best possible hand on a given board is called **the nuts**. To find it, check from the top of the rankings down.

- **Three or more cards of one suit?** A flush is possible, and maybe a straight flush.
- **A pair on the board?** Full houses and four of a kind are possible.
- **Three cards within a five-card span?** A straight is possible.
- **None of these?** The nuts is three of a kind, made with a pocket pair matching the highest board card.

On K-7-2 in three suits, none of the first three apply, so the nuts is {n1.name.toLowerCase()}. On J-10-9 the nuts is a {n2.name.toLowerCase()}, held by king-queen. On 8-8-3 it is {n3.name.toLowerCase()}.

## Draws

A **draw** is a hand that is not strong yet but will be if the right card comes. The two main kinds:

- **Flush draw.** You have four cards of one suit and need a fifth. Possible whenever the board has two or three of a suit.
- **Straight draw.** You have four cards toward a straight. If either end completes it, such as holding 8-7 on a 6-5-K board, it is **open-ended**. If only one rank in the middle works, it is a **gutshot**.

Draws matter even when you do not have one, because your opponent might. A made hand on a board full of draws is in more danger, and usually wants to bet so that draws pay to continue.

## Paired boards

When the board pairs, the ceiling rises. Full houses and four of a kind become possible, and flushes and straights are no longer the nuts.

Pairing also changes existing hands. Suppose you hold 7-6 on a flop of 7-6-2 and have two pair. If the turn and river come king, king, your best five cards are now kings and sevens with a six. Your pair of sixes no longer plays. Any pocket pair from eights up has a better two pair than you, and so does anyone holding a seven with a kicker above your six. Your two pair has been **counterfeited**: the board gave everyone a pair that outranks one of yours.

## Wet and dry

Players describe boards by how many strong hands and draws they allow.

A **dry** board has few. K-7-2 in three suits is the standard example. Of the 1,176 holdings an opponent could have, exactly {dry.straightCombos + dry.flushDrawCombos + dry.openEndedCombos} have a straight, a flush draw or a straight draw with eight outs. Whoever is ahead now is likely to stay ahead.

A **wet** board has many. On J-10-9 with two spades, {wet.straightCombos} holdings already have a straight, {wet.flushDrawCombos} have a flush draw and {wet.openEndedCombos} have a straight draw with eight or more outs. Leads change often.

Why it matters:

- On wet boards, strong made hands should bet larger and more often. Free cards are dangerous.
- On dry boards, small bets do the job. There is little to protect against.
- On wet boards, one pair shrinks in value quickly as more cards come.

<Remember>

- The nuts is the best possible hand on this board. Find it every street
- Three of a suit: flushes. A pair: full houses. Three connected cards: straights
- A draw needs one more card. Flush draws and open-ended straight draws are the strong ones
- A paired board can counterfeit two pair
- Wet boards have many draws, dry boards few. Bet bigger on wet boards with made hands

<Mistakes>

- Valuing a hand by its name instead of by what the board allows
- Missing a possible straight when the board cards are not in order
- Holding a small flush and forgetting that a bigger one is possible
- Giving free cards on wet boards with a hand that is ahead now

</Mistakes>
