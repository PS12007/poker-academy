## How bad normal gets

A **downswing** is the drop from a high point in your results to the lowest point after it. A **break-even stretch** is the number of hands between one high and the next.

Both are surprisingly large for winning players. The engine simulates 2,000 careers of 100,000 hands each at a standard deviation of 90 bb/100.

## Variance or decline?

A long losing stretch has three possible explanations, and they need different responses.

1. **Variance.** Your play is unchanged and the cards have been cold. Response: keep playing well, follow your bankroll rules.
2. **The games got harder, or you got worse.** Response: study, table selection, maybe lower stakes.
3. **Tilt caused by the downswing.** Response: the next lesson.

Results cannot tell these apart over any sample you are likely to have. Decisions can. Review a set of hands from the downswing and judge each decision on its merits, without looking at the outcome. Track a few measurable habits: how often you voluntarily put money in the pot, how often you raise, how often you fold to river bets. If those have shifted, the cause is you.

## Before it happens

- **Know the numbers.** Run the simulator for your own winrate and look at the one-in-twenty case. If that downswing would make you quit, your bankroll or expectations need adjusting now.
- **Pre-commit.** Write down your move-down floor and your stop-loss for a single session.
- **Keep a decision log.** Notes on hard spots let you check decisions later instead of relying on memory, which is biased by results.
- **Expect it.** A downswing that arrives on schedule is much easier to handle than one that feels like a verdict.
