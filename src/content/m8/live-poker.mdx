## Tells are weak evidence

Films make tells look decisive. In practice they are noisy: a player's hands may shake from adrenaline with a monster or from fear with a bluff, and the same gesture means different things for different people.

The right way to use one is as a small update to your range estimate. Bayes' rule makes the size of the update explicit.

<Formula name="updating on a tell">new probability = p × L / (p × L + (1 − p))</Formula>

Here p is your estimate before the tell and L is how much more common the tell is when they bluff than when they have value.

### Common patterns, with caution

- Players who are strong often act relaxed, talk, or look away. Players who are weak often go still and quiet.
- Long pauses followed by a big bet are frequently strong in live games. Quick checks are often weak.
- Glancing at chips after seeing the flop is often interest.
- Reaching for chips before it is their turn can be an attempt to deter a bet.

Each of these is a tendency, not a law, and baseline behavior for the individual matters most. Spend time watching how a player acts in hands that reach showdown.

### Online

There are no faces, but there is timing and, more reliably, sizing. Players who use one size for value and another for bluffs are the clearest exploit online. Timing tells are confused by players handling several tables.

## What is different live

- **Speed.** About 30 hands an hour, against 60 to 100 per table online. Variance spreads over many more hours.
- **Rake and tips.** Live rake is often capped per pot but large relative to small stakes, and tipping adds to it. The next lesson computes the cost.
- **Players.** Live games at low stakes are usually softer: more calling, less bluffing on the river.
- **Information.** You see every hand shown down at the table. Remember them.

## Table selection

Your edge is your skill minus your opponents'. The most effective way to raise your win rate is to sit with weaker players.

- Look for many players seeing the flop and large pots relative to the blinds.
- Prefer seats with loose players to your right, so you act after them.
- Leave tables where several players are clearly better than the rest.

## Giving nothing away

Act the same way every hand: same timing for routine decisions, same placement of chips, same words. Do not show cards voluntarily, and do not discuss hands in progress. The goal is not to be unreadable to experts but to stop leaking information to everyone else.
