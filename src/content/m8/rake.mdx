## The house's share

Cash games make money for the venue through **rake**: a percentage of each pot, usually 2.5 to 10%, up to a **cap**. Many games use "no flop, no drop": pots won before the flop pay nothing. Tournaments charge a fee on the entry instead.

Rake comes out of the pots, so it comes out of winners. It is the single largest reason most players lose in the long run.

## Paying for it per 100 hands

<Formula>rake per 100 hands = raked pots per 100 × min(rate × pot, cap)</Formula>

The percentage is the same and the cost differs by a factor of ten, because caps are fixed in money. At small stakes the cap is many big blinds and almost never binds. At higher stakes it binds on every sizeable pot.

## Who pays

The player who wins the pot pays its rake. Good players win more pots, so they pay more than average. A rough rule: a winning player pays something close to the table's average rake per player, sometimes more. If that is 5 bb/100 and your edge before rake is 6 bb/100, you net 1 bb/100.

## Adjusting for rake

- **Fewer marginal calls preflop.** Calls lead to small flopped pots, where rake takes its full percentage. Hands that are a tiny profit in an unraked game become a loss. Prefer raising, where many pots end preflop and are not raked.
- **Tighter blind defense.** The big blind's widest defends are the most marginal hands of all.
- **Bigger pots or none.** A small pot taken to the flop pays rake without much to win.
- **Choose the game.** A table with lower rake or a better rakeback deal can be worth more than weeks of study.

## Rakeback

Many sites return part of the rake as **rakeback** or rewards. Treat it as part of your winrate: a player paying 5 bb/100 with 30% rakeback gets 1.5 bb/100 back. For marginal winners it can be the difference between winning and losing.

<Remember>

- Rake per 100 = raked pots × min(rate × pot, cap)
- Caps are in money, so rake costs more big blinds at lower stakes
- Small flopped pots pay the highest share. Pots won preflop are often free
- Rake turns marginal calls into losses. Raise or fold more
- Net winrate = winrate before rake − rake paid + rakeback

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Ignoring rake when choosing stakes and games
- Defending the big blind as widely in a raked game as in theory
- Assuming a small edge survives a high-rake game
- Forgetting rakeback when comparing sites

</Mistakes>
