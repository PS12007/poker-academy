## Money that can go to zero without ending your life

A **bankroll** is money set aside only for poker. Rent, food and savings are not in it. Keeping it separate does two things: it lets you track your results honestly, and it lets you apply rules about which stakes you can afford.

The variance lesson in Module 2 showed that a winning player regularly loses for tens of thousands of hands. The bankroll is what survives those stretches.

## Where the guidelines come from

Common advice says 20 to 40 buy-ins for cash games. That advice comes from the risk-of-ruin formula, and you can derive your own number instead of trusting a rule of thumb.

<Formula name="bankroll for a target risk of ruin">bankroll = −(sd² / (2 × winrate)) × ln(risk)</Formula>

All quantities are in big blinds and bb/100. Divide by 100 to get buy-ins.

Two things stand out. The winrate matters enormously: a marginal winner at 1 bb/100 needs several times the bankroll of a solid one. And nobody knows their true winrate precisely, as the variance lesson showed, so it is sensible to plug in a conservative estimate. That is why the common guidance lands at 30 or more buy-ins rather than 15.

## Moving up and down

Decide the rules before you need them.

- **Move up** when you have the buy-ins for the next stake, by your own risk calculation, and your results at the current stake support a positive winrate over a meaningful sample.
- **Take shots**: play a few sessions at the next stake with a small, fixed part of the bankroll. If you lose it, move back down without debate.
- **Move down** at a floor fixed in advance. Moving down is not failure. It is the rule that makes the risk-of-ruin numbers true, because the formula assumes a stake you can afford.

With a move-down rule in place, true ruin becomes very unlikely even with a smaller bankroll, because losses shrink as the stakes do.

## Tournaments

Tournament variance is of a different order. A typical winning player cashes perhaps 15% of the time and makes most of their profit from rare final tables. Losing streaks of 100 or more entries are routine. Guidelines of 100 to 200 buy-ins for multi-table tournaments, and more for very large fields, reflect that. Single-table events with flatter payouts need fewer.

## Life roll and bankroll

Professional players need living expenses kept apart from the bankroll, usually six months or more. Withdrawing from the bankroll to pay bills during a downswing is the most common way skilled players go broke.

<Remember>

- A bankroll is money for poker only
- Bankroll for a target risk = −(sd² / (2 × winrate)) × ln(risk)
- Use a conservative winrate. Most cash players need 30 or more buy-ins
- Set move-up and move-down rules in advance
- Tournaments need 100 or more buy-ins
- Keep living expenses separate

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Playing stakes the bankroll cannot support because you feel you are better than the game
- Moving up after a short winning streak
- Refusing to move down during a downswing
- Paying living costs from the bankroll

</Mistakes>
