Judging decisions by results makes you abandon good plays that lost and repeat bad plays that won. The name for this is **results-oriented thinking**, and it is the root of most tilt.

## What tilt looks like

**Tilt** is playing worse than you know how to because of emotion. It comes in several forms.

- **Loss tilt:** chasing losses with looser calls and bigger bluffs to get even.
- **Injustice tilt:** after a bad beat, feeling owed and playing as if the next hand should make up for it.
- **Winner's tilt:** after a good run, playing loose and overconfident.
- **Entitlement tilt:** anger at weaker players winning, and trying to punish them.
- **Fatigue:** decisions decay late in long sessions without any single trigger.

The early signs are physical and behavioral: faster decisions, replaying a hand in your head, thoughts about getting even, playing hands you normally fold.

## Rules made when calm

Willpower in the moment is unreliable, because tilt impairs the judgment you would use to notice it. Set rules beforehand.

- **Stop-loss.** End the session after losing a fixed number of buy-ins.
- **Time limit.** End after a fixed time, regardless of results.
- **Trigger rule.** After a large pot lost, sit out for two orbits or take a walk.
- **No moving up** in a session that started at another stake.

## The session review

After a session, review decisions rather than results.

1. **Pick hands by difficulty**, not pot size. Mark close spots during play.
2. **Hide the outcome** if your software allows. Decide what you would do before you look.
3. **Use the tools.** Count combos, compute the price, run the equity. Most spots reduce to a pot odds or EV comparison you already know how to do.
4. **Write down one change.** A single concrete adjustment is worth more than a page of feelings.
5. **Log the emotional state**, briefly. Patterns in when you tilt are useful.

A good review leaves you with the same verdict on a hand whether it won or lost.

<Remember>

- Tilt is emotion-driven play below your skill level, after wins or losses
- Judge decisions by EV, not by outcome
- Watch for speed, chasing, and playing hands you normally fold
- Set stop-loss, time and trigger rules in advance
- Review hard spots with the result hidden

</Remember>
