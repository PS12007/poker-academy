## Four cards, four blockers

A **blocker** is a card in your hand that the opponent cannot have. In Hold'em you hold two, so blockers are a small edge. In Omaha you hold four, and strong hands are made so often that knowing what your opponent **cannot** have is worth a lot.

## The ace of the suit

The most important blocker in Omaha is the **ace of the flush suit**.

<Scene hands={['AsJd8c4h']} board="Ks9s3s" labels={['You hold one spade, the ace']} />

The board has three spades. You do not have a flush, because you need two spades. But **nobody** can have the nut flush, because it needs the ace of spades, and you are holding it.

That makes the bare ace a powerful bluffing card. If you bet big, your opponent knows you might have the nut flush, and they can never be sure their flush is good. If they hold a smaller flush, they face a hard decision.

## Set blockers

Pairs in your hand block sets. Hold K-K-x-x on a king-high flop and only one king is left in the deck. The opponent cannot have top set, and you have it. Hold 9-9 on a board with a nine and the opponent has at most one combination of nine-nine left.

The flip side: if you hold a hand that **wants** the opponent to have something, do not block it. When bluff catching, you would rather hold cards that block their value hands than cards that block their busted draws.

## Using blockers

Blockers are best for choosing **between** similar hands, not for making big decisions by themselves.

- **Bluff** with hands that block the nuts, such as the bare ace of the flush suit.
- **Call** more readily when you block the opponent's best hands.
- **Fold** more readily when you block their bluffs, for example when you hold the cards of the straight draw they would have missed.

Remember: blocking the nut flush does not block the second-nut flush, a full house, or a straight. A careful opponent can still call.

<Remember>

- You hold four cards, so you block more than in Hold'em
- The ace of the flush suit means nobody else has the nut flush
- Your pairs block sets of the same rank
- Bluff with nut blockers. Call when you block their value hands
- Blockers break close decisions. They do not override the board

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Bluffing a three-flush board with no ace of the suit
- Forgetting that your own pair makes their top set unlikely
- Calling with hands that block the bluffs you hope to catch
- Thinking a blocker means the opponent has nothing

</Mistakes>
