## The pot is the limit

In pot-limit games you can bet any amount from the minimum up to **the size of the pot**. That is the whole idea. The only tricky part is raising, because the pot changes when you call.

## The rule for raising

When you face a bet, the maximum raise works in two steps.

1. **Pretend to call.** Add your call to the pot.
2. **Raise the size of that new pot.**

**Quick rule:** facing a bet B into a pot P, the max raise is to **3 × B + P**. Here: 3 × 50 + 50 = 200. It works every time there is a single bet in front of you.

## Preflop

The blinds count as bets. With blinds of 1 and 2, the first player to act can raise to at most {maxRaiseTo(3, 2, 0)}: call 2 (pot becomes 5), then raise 5 more. That is 3.5 big blinds, which is why "raise pot" preflop is so common in PLO.

## The pot grows fast

Limits do not keep pots small for long. A pot-sized bet and call **triples** the pot. A pot-sized raise is bigger still.

Starting from a pot of 10, one pot bet and call makes 30, then 90, then 270. Three streets of pot bets get 130 from each player into the middle. So with 100 big blind stacks, PLO pots regularly end all-in, just a street or two later than in no-limit.

You also cannot bluff someone off a hand with an enormous overbet. The most you can put in is the pot, so big draws often have the right price to continue.

<Remember>

- You can bet up to the size of the pot
- To raise the max: call first, then raise the new pot
- Facing bet B into pot P: max raise is to 3B + P
- Preflop pot raise with blinds 1 and 2 is to 7
- Each pot bet and call triples the pot, so stacks still go in

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Forgetting to add your call before raising the pot
- Leaving the opponent's bet out of the pot
- Thinking limits mean small pots
- Saying "pot" in a live game without knowing the number. Work it out or ask the dealer

</Mistakes>
