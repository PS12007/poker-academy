## Favorites are smaller

In Hold'em, a pair of aces beats a pair of kings about <Eq a="AsAh" b="KsKh" /> of the time. Big edges like that are normal.

In Omaha, edges shrink. These numbers come from the engine, not a book.

The best possible starting hand, double-suited aces and kings, is only about a 60% favorite against a good rundown. Bare aces against a strong double-suited rundown is close to a coin flip.

## Why

Each Omaha hand is really **six two-card hands**. The underdog does not need one lucky card. It has six different pairs looking for a straight, a flush or two pair, so it gets there far more often.

The more connected and suited a hand is, the more of its six pairs are live. That is why rundowns do so well against aces.

## What this means for strategy

- **Preflop all-ins win little.** Even your best hands are rarely huge favorites. Getting all-in before the flop gains much less than in Hold'em.
- **Postflop is where the money is.** After the flop, hands separate sharply: someone has the nuts and someone is drawing dead. Good players keep pots smaller preflop and win them after the flop.
- **Position matters even more.** With so much play after the flop, acting last is worth a lot.
