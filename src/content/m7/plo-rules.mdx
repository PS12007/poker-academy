## Same game, four cards

Pot-Limit Omaha, or PLO, is played just like Hold'em: same blinds, same four betting rounds, same five community cards. Two things change.

1. **You get four hole cards instead of two.**
2. **You must use exactly two of them, and exactly three board cards.**

There is also a betting difference: you can only bet up to the size of the pot. That gets its own lesson next.

## The two-plus-three rule

This rule is the whole game. Every hand is made of **two cards from your hand and three from the board**. Not one from your hand. Not three. Two.

That sounds simple, and it still catches everyone at first. Here are the three classic traps.

**A four-flush board.** The board has four hearts. In Hold'em, any heart in your hand makes a flush. In Omaha you can only use three board hearts, so you need **two** hearts in your hand.

**Four of a kind on board.** The board is 7-7-7-7-K. In Hold'em everyone has quads. In Omaha you can only use three of the sevens, so the best anyone can do is a full house.

**A four-straight board.** The board is 5-6-7-8-K. In Hold'em a single 9 or 4 makes a straight. In Omaha you need two cards that fit, like 9-T or 9-4.

## Reading your hand quickly

A good habit: before thinking about anything else, look at your four cards as **six two-card pairs**. A hand like A-K-Q-J contains A-K, A-Q, A-J, K-Q, K-J and Q-J. Each pair is a possible "Hold'em hand", and you get to use the best one with the best three board cards.

That is why PLO hands hit the board so often: you have six chances instead of one. It is also why big hands are common and the nuts matter so much, as later lessons show.

<Remember>

- Four hole cards. Use exactly two of them and exactly three board cards
- A flush needs two of the suit in your hand
- Four of a kind on board does not play. Three of them do
- A straight needs two hole cards that fit
- Your four cards are six two-card hands at once

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Counting a single suited card as a flush
- Thinking a board straight or board quads plays for you
- Reading four hole cards as if all four could be used
- Forgetting a card you hold blocks the same card for everyone else

</Mistakes>
