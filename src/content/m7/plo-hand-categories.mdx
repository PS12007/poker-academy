## So many hands

There are {int(counts.total)} possible four-card starting hands. Treating hands that only differ by which suit is which as the same still leaves {int(counts.distinct)}. Nobody memorizes a chart of that. Instead, you judge a hand by a few features.

## What makes a hand good

A good Omaha hand has **all four cards working together**. Each of your six two-card pairs should be useful.

**1. Connected.** Cards close in rank make straights. A **rundown** like 9-8-7-6 or J-T-9-8 can make many straights and the big "wrap" draws you will meet later.

**2. Suited, ideally double suited.** One suited pair gives you a flush draw. Two suited pairs (**double suited**, like A♠K♠Q♥J♥) give you two. Flushes made with an ace are the most valuable, because they are the nuts.

**3. High.** When two players make the same kind of hand, the higher one wins. High cards make the top straight and the top set.

**4. No danglers.** A **dangler** is a card that does not connect with the others, like the 4 in A-K-Q-4. It wastes one quarter of your hand.

The best hands have all four: A♠A♥K♠Q♥ is high, double suited, and every card connects.

## Hands that look good but are not

- **Four of one suit** (K♠Q♠8♠5♠). You can only use two spades, so you have one flush draw, and you are holding two of your own outs.
- **Three of a rank** (8-8-8-3). You can only use two eights, and the third one kills your own set outs.
- **Pairs with nothing else** (Q-Q-7-2 rainbow). Top set sometimes, but often second best.

## Aces are not king here

In Hold'em, A-A is a huge favorite against anything. In Omaha it is much less so. Your opponent has six two-card pairs, so they make two pair, straights and flushes far more often. An **unimproved pair of aces** is usually beaten by the river in a big pot.

Aces are still the best pair to hold, because they make top set and block the opponent's best hands. But A-A-x-x is only as good as its other two cards. A♠A♥K♠Q♥ is excellent. A♠A♥7♦2♣ is a much weaker hand that often wins only a small pot.
