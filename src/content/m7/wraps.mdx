## Straight draws on steroids

In Hold'em the biggest pure straight draw has 8 outs. In Omaha, because you hold four cards, you can have cards **below and above** the board at the same time. A straight draw that uses three or four of your cards this way is called a **wrap**, and it can have up to 20 outs.

How to count by hand: list every rank that makes a straight with two of your cards, then subtract the cards of those ranks you can already see.

<Example title="The 20-out wrap">

Hand J-T-7-6, flop 9-8-2.

- **Q** makes Q-J-T-9-8, using your J-T.
- **J** makes J-T-9-8-7, using your T-7.
- **T** makes T-9-8-7-6, using your 7-6.
- **7** makes T-9-8-7-6, using your T-6.
- **6** makes T-9-8-7-6, using your T-7.
- **5** makes 9-8-7-6-5, using your 7-6.

That is six ranks: Q, J, T, 7, 6, 5. Four of each is 24 cards. You hold one J, one T, one 7 and one 6, so 24 − 4 = **20 outs**.

</Example>

## Not every out is equal

**Nut outs** give you the best possible hand. **Non-nut outs** make a straight that can lose.

- **The low end.** On 9-8-2, a 5 gives J-T-7-6 the 9-to-5 straight. Anyone holding T-7 or J-T has a higher straight. Low-end outs are worth less.
- **Flush boards.** If the board has two of a suit and the turn or river brings a third, your straight may already be beaten by a flush.
- **Paired boards.** If the board pairs, full houses are possible and straights shrink in value.

When you count, ask: "if this card comes, am I likely to have the best hand?" Drawing hard to second-best hands is the biggest money leak in PLO.

<Remember>

- Wraps use three or four hole cards around the board
- On 9-8-2: J-T has 8 outs, Q-J-T 13, J-T-7 17, J-T-7-6 20
- 45 unseen cards on the flop, 44 on the turn
- A 20-out wrap makes a straight about 70% of the time by the river
- Prefer nut outs. Low-end and flush-board outs are weaker

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Counting Hold'em style and missing half the outs
- Forgetting to remove the cards in your own hand
- Treating every straight out as a winning card
- Drawing to the low end on boards with flush draws

</Mistakes>
