<Plain>In Omaha, strong hands are common, so second-best hands lose big pots. Play big pots with the best possible hand or a draw to it, and keep the pot small otherwise.</Plain>

## Big hands everywhere

Each player has six two-card combinations, so by the river someone often has a straight, a flush or a full house. The result: **hands that are strong in Hold'em are weak in Omaha.**

- **Top pair** is usually a small hand. Check, call small bets, fold to aggression.
- **Two pair** looks strong but is beaten by every set and is behind many big draws. It is the classic PLO trap.
- **Sets** are strong. Top set is very strong. They still lose to wraps that hit.
- **The nuts** is what wins big pots.

## Play for the nuts

The rule that keeps PLO players out of trouble: **put your stack in with the nuts or a strong draw to the nuts.**

- A flush draw with the ace is worth much more than one with the king.
- A straight draw to the top straight is worth more than one to the bottom.
- A set of kings is better than a set of fives on the same board.

When you are not drawing to the nuts, keep the pot small and be ready to fold.

## Freerolls

Sometimes two players hold the same made hand, such as the nut straight. If one of them also has a flush draw, that player is **freerolling**: they cannot lose, since the worst case is a split pot, and they win the whole pot if the flush comes.

Freerolls are common in Omaha because hands carry so many extra draws. When you have the nuts, look for the extras in your other two cards. When you have the nuts and nothing else, be a little careful about raising against an opponent who might hold the same hand plus a draw.

## Sets against wraps

Top set against a big wrap is one of the most common big pots in PLO. Check the numbers in the calculator. On K♥9♣6♦, top set K-K-Q-3 against the wrap J-T-8-7 is about a coin flip.

Add a flush draw to the wrap (make it J♥T♥ and turn the 9 into the 9♥) and the wrap becomes the favorite. This is why sets should bet and raise: they want the money in while they are ahead, before the draws come in.

## Planning with SPR

Pot-limit makes it easy to plan. A pot bet and call triples the pot.

- With an SPR around 1 to 2, one pot bet is all-in. Commit with any strong hand or big draw.
- With an SPR around {num(spr(100, 20), 0)}, a pot bet on the flop and one on the turn get stacks in. Your flop decision is effectively a decision about the whole stack.
- With a high SPR, keep the pot small with hands that are not the nuts, and save big pots for big hands.

<Mistakes>

- Stacking off with two pair
- Drawing to non-nut flushes in big pots
- Slowplaying sets on draw-heavy boards
- Ignoring freerolls on both sides
