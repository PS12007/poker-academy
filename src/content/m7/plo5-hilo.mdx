## Five-card Omaha

**PLO5** gives each player five hole cards instead of four. The two-plus-three rule does not change: exactly two from your hand, three from the board.

Five cards make {pairs5} two-card combinations instead of 6. There are {int(plo5)} possible starting hands. More combinations means:

- **Even bigger hands.** Straights, flushes and full houses are made more often, so the nuts matters even more.
- **Even closer equities.** Preflop favorites are smaller still.
- **More blockers.** Five cards block more of the opponent's hands.

Play it like four-card Omaha, only stricter: draw to the nuts, and treat sets and non-nut flushes with more caution.

## Omaha hi-lo

In **Omaha hi-lo** (also called Omaha eight-or-better), the pot is split in half.

- **High half:** the best normal poker hand, as usual.
- **Low half:** the lowest five-card hand made of **five different cards of rank 8 or lower**. Aces count as low. Straights and flushes do not hurt a low hand. The best low is A-2-3-4-5, called the wheel.

The two-plus-three rule applies separately to each half: you can use two hole cards for the high and a different two for the low.

If no player has a qualifying low, the high hand wins the whole pot.

### Scooping and quartering

- **Scoop:** winning both halves. This is the goal. Winning half a pot you built with two other players often just returns your money.
- **Quartered:** sharing the low half with another player who has the same low. You get a quarter of the pot, which can turn a call into a loss.

The best starting hands in hi-lo contain an **ace with a deuce** (for the nut low), and are also suited or high enough to win the high. A-A-2-3 double suited is the dream.

<Remember>

- PLO5: five hole cards, still exactly two used, ten two-card combinations
- PLO5 hands run even closer, and the nuts matters even more
- Hi-lo splits the pot: best high and best eight-or-better low
- Aces are low for the low, straights and flushes do not count against it
- Aim to scoop. Beware being quartered

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Using three hole cards in PLO5
- Overvaluing non-nut hands because you have five cards
- Playing hi-lo hands that can only win half the pot
- Calling big bets for a low that is likely to be quartered

</Mistakes>
