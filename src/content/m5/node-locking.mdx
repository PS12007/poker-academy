## From the perfect opponent to the real one

An equilibrium tells you how to play against someone who makes no mistakes. Real opponents make the same few mistakes over and over. To profit from them you need to know two things: what the mistake is, and what the best response to it looks like.

**Node locking** answers the second. You take a solved spot, fix one decision for one player to what real people do, and re-solve everything else. The solver is now finding the best response to an imperfect opponent, and its recommendations often change a great deal.

You can do the essential arithmetic by hand, because you already have the formulas.

## Locking the caller

Take the pot-sized river bet. At equilibrium the defender calls {pct(mdf(1, 1), 0)} and the bettor's air is indifferent.

Two things move at once.

**Against an over-folder**, bluffs print money, so every hand with no showdown value becomes a bluff. Your value range should tighten or stay the same, since you are paid less often.

**Against an over-caller**, bluffs lose, so you stop. Value bets earn more, so you bet hands you would have checked. The thin value bet is the main weapon against players who call too much.

## Locking the bettor

Now lock the other side. At equilibrium a pot-sized river bet is {pct(bluffFraction(1, 1), 0)} bluffs, and a bluff catcher is indifferent.

Against an under-bluffer, MDF is the wrong guide. It was derived for a balanced bettor. If the bluffs are not there, folding more than MDF is correct, and the more they under-bluff, the closer you get to folding everything that cannot beat a value bet.

## Exploits travel backward

A change at one node changes the value of every path leading to it.

Suppose a population folds too much to river bets after calling the flop and turn. Then:

- River bluffs are profitable, as above.
- So **turn bets** with hands that will bluff the river become more profitable, since they lead to a good river spot.
- So does the **flop bet** that starts the line.

The reverse holds too. If opponents call rivers too much, three-street bluffs lose at the end, and you should bluff less on the earlier streets, keeping only bluffs with real equity. A solver with a locked node recomputes all of this at once. By hand, the rule is to follow the money back up the tree.

There is a second effect on the opponent's side. If a player never check-raises the flop, their checking and calling range is uncapped in theory but in practice their strong hands are all in the calling range. That changes how you should size later bets against it.

## Common population tendencies

These patterns are widely observed in low and middle stakes games. Treat them as starting assumptions to be confirmed, not facts about any one player.

| Tendency | Best response |
|---|---|
| Calls too much before the flop | Open and 3-bet for value more, bluff less preflop, bet bigger |
| Folds too much to 3-bets | 3-bet bluff more with blocker hands |
| Folds too much to flop bets | Bet small with the whole range more often |
| Rarely check-raises | Bet thinner and more often, since bets are seldom punished |
| Calls flop and turn too wide, folds river to big bets | Barrel three streets with bluffs |
| Calls rivers too much | Stop river bluffs, value bet thin |
| Bluffs rivers too little, especially with raises | Fold bluff catchers to big bets and raises |
| Bets big only with strong hands | Fold more to big bets, call small ones wider |

## Limits

- **Sample size.** A frequency read needs many observations. Ten hands tell you little, as the variance lesson showed.
- **Individuals differ.** The population is an average of tight players and loose ones.
- **You become exploitable.** Never bluffing against a caller is right until the caller notices and starts folding. Against opponents who adapt, move back toward balance.
- **Start small.** A read supports a shift in the direction of the exploit. The size of the shift should match how sure you are.

<Remember>

- Node locking fixes one decision to a realistic strategy and solves the best response around it
- Over-folder: bluff more. Over-caller: stop bluffing and value bet thinner
- Under-bluffer: fold bluff catchers, ignoring MDF
- An exploit on a late street changes the value of the earlier bets that lead there
- Population reads are a starting point. Confirm them, and expect opponents to adapt

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Bluffing a calling station because theory says to bluff
- Calling at MDF against players who never bluff
- Exploiting hard on a tiny sample
- Adjusting one street and forgetting the streets before it

</Mistakes>
