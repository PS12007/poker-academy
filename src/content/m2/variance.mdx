## Two numbers describe a player

Play a positive EV decision once and anything can happen. Play it ten thousand times and the average pulls toward the EV. In between is where poker players live, and it is a noisier place than most expect.

Results in cash games are summarized by two numbers.

- **Winrate**: average profit in big blinds per 100 hands (bb/100). This is the skill part. A strong winner might make 5 bb/100. Most players are below zero after rake.
- **Standard deviation**: how much a typical 100-hand stretch differs from that average. For six-handed no-limit Hold'em it is usually between 75 and 100 bb/100.

Put those side by side. A good player expects to win 5 big blinds over the next 100 hands, give or take 90. The noise is eighteen times the signal.

## How noise shrinks, slowly

Over more hands the two quantities grow at different rates.

<Formula name="after N hands">expected win = winrate × N/100 &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; spread = sd × √(N/100)</Formula>

Expected winnings grow in proportion to N. The spread grows only with the square root of N. So the signal does eventually win, but it takes a long time.

Read the last column. After 10,000 hands your measured winrate is uncertain by ±{num(se(10000), 0)} bb/100, and that is only one standard error. A two standard error range is twice as wide. A player who has won 8 bb/100 over 10,000 hands could easily be a small loser. After 100,000 hands the uncertainty is still ±{num(se(100000), 1)}, which is about the size of a good winrate.

This is the sense in which results lie in the short run. A week, a month, even a few months of play cannot tell a winning player from a losing one.

## Going broke

A winning player with too small a bankroll can still be wiped out by an ordinary downswing. For a player who never moves down in stakes, the chance of eventually losing a bankroll of B big blinds is

<Formula name="risk of ruin">exp(−2 × winrate × B / sd²)</Formula>

At 5 bb/100 with a standard deviation of 90, a bankroll of 20 buy-ins (2,000 bb) has a {pct(ror20)} risk of ruin. Fifty buy-ins brings it down to {pct(ror50)}. To hold it to 5% takes {int(bankrollForRisk(5, sd, 0.05))} bb. Module 7 on bankroll management builds on this formula.

## What to do about it

Since results are unreliable, judge yourself by decisions.

- Review hands for whether the play was right, not whether it won.
- Treat any conclusion from fewer than tens of thousands of hands with suspicion, including good news.
- Keep enough bankroll that a normal downswing cannot end your game.
- Expect losing months. They are part of the distribution, not evidence against it.

<Remember>

- Winrate is the average in bb/100. Standard deviation is the typical swing, around 75 to 100 bb/100
- Expected winnings grow with N. The spread grows with √N
- Uncertainty in a measured winrate is sd / √(N/100)
- To halve the uncertainty you need four times the hands
- Risk of ruin = exp(−2 × winrate × bankroll / sd²)
- Judge decisions, not results

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Drawing conclusions about skill from a few thousand hands
- Moving up in stakes after a hot streak
- Changing a sound strategy during a downswing
- Playing with a bankroll that an ordinary swing can erase

</Mistakes>
