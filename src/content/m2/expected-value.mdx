## An average over everything that can happen

**Expected value**, or EV, is the average result of a decision if you could repeat it a very large number of times. To compute it, list every outcome, multiply each by its probability, and add.

<Formula name="expected value">EV = Σ probability × result</Formula>

A coin is flipped. Heads you win 11, tails you lose 10. The EV is 0.5 × 11 − 0.5 × 10 = {signed(coin)}. You will never win 0.50 on a flip. You will win 11 or lose 10. But take the bet a thousand times and you will be ahead by about 500.

Poker is a long series of bets like that one, with harder probabilities. A winning player is someone who keeps choosing the option with the highest EV and accepts whatever each individual result turns out to be.

## The zero point

EV is measured from the moment of the decision. Chips you put in earlier are no longer yours. They are in the pot, and the pot is what you are playing for.

This makes the EV of **folding** exactly zero. You win nothing more and lose nothing more. Every other option is judged against that. A call with positive EV is better than folding, and a call with negative EV is worse.

The same idea disposes of a common error. Having already invested in a pot is not a reason to continue. The money is counted once, as part of the pot you can win. Counting it again as something to protect is the **sunk cost** mistake.

## EV of a call

With no further betting, a call has two outcomes.

<Formula name="calling">EV = equity × pot − (1 − equity) × call</Formula>

The pot is 150 including a 50 bet, and you have 30% equity: 0.30 × 150 − 0.70 × 50 = {signed(callEv)}. Setting this formula to zero and solving for equity gives the pot odds formula from the earlier lesson. Pot odds are the break-even case of an EV calculation.

## EV of a bluff

A pure bluff has no chance of winning at showdown. It wins the pot when the opponent folds and loses the bet when they call.

<Formula name="pure bluff">EV = fold% × pot − (1 − fold%) × bet</Formula>

## EV as a decision tree

Most real decisions have branches. A **semi-bluff** is a bet with a hand that is probably behind but can improve. It has three outcomes: they fold, they call and you win, they call and you lose.

<Formula name="shoving">EV = fold% × pot + (1 − fold%) × (equity × final pot − risk)</Formula>

## Comparing options

EV only means something as a comparison. At each decision, estimate the EV of every option available and pick the largest. Sometimes the best option still has negative EV, such as paying the small blind, and sometimes two options are both positive and you are choosing the better one.

You will rarely know the probabilities exactly. That does not make the method useless. A rough estimate fed through the right formula beats a guess with no structure, and the formulas show which input matters most. In the shove above, the answer depends heavily on fold equity and hardly at all on a few points of card equity.

<Remember>

- EV is the sum of each result times its probability
- Folding has an EV of zero. Money already in the pot is not yours
- Call: equity × pot − (1 − equity) × call
- Bluff: fold% × pot − (1 − fold%) × bet
- Shove: fold% × pot + (1 − fold%) × (equity × final pot − risk)
- Pick the highest EV option and ignore single results

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Continuing because of chips already invested
- Judging a play by one outcome
- Forgetting the fold branch when evaluating a bet with a drawing hand
- Risking a large amount to win a small pot without enough fold equity

</Mistakes>
