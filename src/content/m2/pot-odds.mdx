## The price of a call

When you face a bet, you are being offered a wager. You put in the amount of the bet. If you win, you collect everything in the pot. **Pot odds** are the terms of that wager, and they tell you how often you need to win for calling to break even.

<Formula name="break-even equity">equity needed = call / (pot + call)</Formula>

Here **pot** means everything you can win: the chips that were in the middle plus the bet you are facing. **Call** is what you must put in.

The reasoning behind it: after you call, the final pot is pot + call. Your call is a share of that final pot. If you win more often than your share, you profit.

## Equity

**Equity** is your share of the pot on average, if the remaining cards were dealt with no more betting. If you would win 35 times in 100, your equity is 35%. For a drawing hand it is close to the probability of hitting, which is why the last two lessons came first.

The decision rule is one comparison:

- Equity above the break-even figure: calling makes money.
- Equity below it: calling loses money.

## Odds and percentages

Players often state the price as a ratio. Pot 150, call 50 is **3 to 1**: you win three units for each one you risk. To turn a ratio into the equity needed:

<Formula>x to 1 → 1 / (x + 1)</Formula>

So 3 to 1 needs 25%, 2 to 1 needs 33.3%, and 4 to 1 needs 20%.

## Common sizes

Bet sizes are usually thought of as fractions of the pot, and each fraction has a fixed break-even equity. These are worth memorizing.

| Bet size | Odds you get | Equity needed |
|---|---|---|
| One third of the pot | 4 to 1 | {pct(potOdds(4, 1))} |
| Half the pot | 3 to 1 | {pct(potOdds(3, 1))} |
| Two thirds of the pot | 2.5 to 1 | {pct(potOdds(2.5, 1))} |
| The pot | 2 to 1 | {pct(potOdds(2, 1))} |
| Twice the pot | 1.5 to 1 | {pct(potOdds(1.5, 1))} |

The required equity never reaches 50%, however large the bet. A bet has to be infinitely large before the caller needs to be a favorite.

## Putting it together

On the turn, the pot is 100 and your opponent bets 100. You hold a flush draw with nine clean outs and no other way to win.

1. Outs to probability: 9 / 46 = {pct(fd.nextCardFromTurn)}.
2. Price: 100 / (200 + 100) = {pct(full)}.
3. Compare: {pct(fd.nextCardFromTurn)} is well short of {pct(full)}. Fold.

If the bet were 25 instead, you would need 25 / (125 + 25) = {pct(potOdds(125, 25))}, and the same draw becomes a profitable call. The cards did not change. The price did.

<Remember>

- Equity needed = call / (pot + call), where the pot includes their bet
- Third pot needs 20%, half pot 25%, two thirds 28.6%, full pot 33.3%
- x to 1 odds need 1 / (x + 1)
- Call when your equity beats the price. Fold when it does not
- Pot odds alone assume no further betting

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Leaving the opponent's bet out of the pot
- Comparing a price with the two-card probability when you are only buying one card
- Calling because the bet is small in chips rather than checking it against the pot
- Folding hands with plenty of equity to small bets

</Mistakes>
