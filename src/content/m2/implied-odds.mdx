## Money that is not in the pot yet

Pot odds compare your call with what is in the pot now. That is exact when the call ends the betting. Usually it does not. If you call on the flop or turn, there are more betting rounds to come, and what happens in them changes the value of the call.

**Implied odds** account for money you expect to win later, on the times you make your hand. If you hit a hidden draw and your opponent pays off a river bet, that bet was part of your reward for calling earlier, even though it was not in the pot at the time.

## How much do you need?

Take a call that is wrong on direct odds and ask what would make it right. Let the extra amount you win after hitting be X.

<Formula name="break-even extra">X = call × (1 − equity) / equity − pot</Formula>

It comes from setting the EV to zero: equity × (pot + X) − (1 − equity) × call = 0.

<Example title="A flush draw facing a pot-sized turn bet">

The pot is 100, your opponent bets 100, and you have nine outs with one card to come.

## When implied odds are real

The extra money has to come from somewhere. Four things decide whether it will.

1. **Stack depth.** You cannot win what is not there. With little behind, implied odds are close to zero. When either player is all-in they are exactly zero.
2. **How hidden your hand is.** A flush is obvious when the third suited card arrives. A set, or a straight made with a gutshot, is not. Hidden hands get paid.
3. **How strong your made hand is.** You want to be drawing to the nuts, so that when big money goes in you are the one ahead.
4. **The opponent.** Someone who cannot fold top pair pays draws off. Someone careful does not.

<Example title="Calling a raise with a small pair">

You hold a small pocket pair and face a raise to 3 big blinds. The pot is 5.5bb including the raise. A pair flops a set or better {pct(pSet)} of the time, which is {num(oddsAgainst(pSet), 1)} to 1 against.

When you miss you will usually have to give up. Treating the hand as set or nothing, the extra you need when you hit is 3 × {num(1 - pSet, 3)} / {num(pSet, 3)} − 5.5 = {num(setNeed, 1)}bb.

That sounds small, but it is an average over every time you flop a set, including the times the raiser has nothing and folds, and the times your set loses. This is why players use a margin: a common guideline is to want effective stacks of at least 15 times the raise before calling only to hit a set. The guideline is a rule of thumb, not a derived number. The break-even figure above is the derived number, and the gap between them is your allowance for not getting paid.

</Example>

A gutshot facing a half-pot bet on the flop needs {num(gutNeed, 0)} extra per 50 called, more than twice the pot. Gutshots are rarely worth calling for their own sake.

## Reverse implied odds

Sometimes the future money flows the other way. **Reverse implied odds** are what you stand to lose later when you make your hand and it is second best.

Typical cases:

- **Dominated top pair.** You hold K-9, the flop comes king-high, and you pay three streets to K-Q.
- **Non-nut flush draws.** You make a small flush and run into a bigger one.
- **The low end of a straight.**
- **Drawing on a paired board**, where a flush or straight can already be losing to a full house.

In each case the hand has decent equity on paper and still loses money, because it wins small pots and loses big ones. When you estimate a call, subtract for these. A draw to the nuts and a draw to the third-best hand may have the same number of outs and very different values.

<Remember>

- Implied odds add future winnings to the pot. Reverse implied odds subtract future losses
- Extra needed = call × (1 − equity) / equity − pot
- Implied odds need deep stacks, a hidden hand, a strong made hand and an opponent who pays
- When someone is all-in, implied odds are zero
- A pocket pair flops a set or better {pct(pSet)} of the time
- Draws to the nuts are worth far more than draws to second best

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Using implied odds as an excuse for any call
- Assuming the opponent will pay off an obvious flush
- Set mining with short stacks
- Ignoring reverse implied odds with weak top pairs and small flush draws

</Mistakes>
