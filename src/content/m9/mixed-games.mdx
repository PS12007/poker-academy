## Many games in one

A **mixed game** rotates through several variants, switching every orbit or every few hands. The best-known rotation is **HORSE**:

| Letter | Game | Type |
|---|---|---|
| H | Hold'em | Community cards, high |
| O | Omaha hi-lo | Community cards, split pot |
| R | Razz | Stud, low |
| S | Seven card stud | Stud, high |
| E | Stud eight-or-better | Stud, split pot |

Other rotations add **pot-limit Omaha**, **2-7 triple draw** (a draw game where you can replace cards three times and the lowest hand wins, with aces high and straights counting against you), and **badugi** (a four-card draw game for low with all different suits). An "8-game" or "dealer's choice" mixes several more.

Mixed games are usually **fixed limit**, apart from the no-limit and pot-limit games in the mix. That keeps the stakes even across games and makes odds and position matter more than big bluffs.

## What stays the same

Each game has its own rules, but the decisions are built from the same pieces you have learned here.

- **Pot odds and EV.** A call needs equity above the price, in every game.
- **Counting outs and card removal.** In stud you see more dead cards, in draw games you see fewer.
- **Position.** Acting last is worth money in every variant.
- **Range thinking.** Each action narrows what a player can hold.
- **Bankroll and tilt.** Variance does not care which game you are playing.

## How to approach a new game

1. **Learn the rules cold**, including how hands are ranked and who acts first.
2. **Learn starting hands**, which matter most in fixed-limit games.
3. **Recount your odds.** Outs and probabilities change with the deck and the number of cards.
4. **Play smaller stakes** for that game until your results and decisions settle.
5. **Find the weak games of others.** Most mixed-game profit comes from opponents who are strong in one or two games and weak in the rest.

- Learn new games at lower stakes first

- Playing big in your weakest game
