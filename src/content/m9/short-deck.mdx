## Hold'em with fewer cards

**Short deck** (also called 6+ Hold'em) is played like Hold'em with two hole cards and five on the board. The difference is the deck: all the twos, threes, fours and fives are removed, leaving 36 cards.

Three things change.

1. **A flush beats a full house** in most games.
2. **The ace plays low in A-6-7-8-9**, the lowest straight.
3. **Some games rank three of a kind above a straight.** Check the house rules before you play.

Games often use an **ante** from everyone instead of blinds, with the button posting extra.

## Why a flush beats a full house

The ranking of hands is the order of rarity, as in Module 1. Removing sixteen low cards changes that order. With only nine cards in each suit, flushes become much harder to make, while full houses become easier because each rank is a bigger share of the deck.

In the full deck a flush is rarer than a straight and more common than a full house. In the short deck it drops below the full house in frequency, so it moves up in the ranking.

## What changes in play

- **More action, closer equities.** Every card is six or higher, so hands connect with the board more often and draws come in more often.
- **Draws hit more often.** An open-ended straight draw still has 8 outs, but from only 31 unseen cards on the flop instead of 47, so each card is a 26% shot instead of 17%.
- **Pairs are less dominant.** Aces against kings is still a big edge, but small pairs fall behind quickly.
- **Flush draws lose value.** With four of your suit visible, only five more of that suit remain in the deck, so a flush draw has 5 outs instead of 9.

Treat it as a different game with familiar rules, and recount odds before trusting Hold'em numbers.
