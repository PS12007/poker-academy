## Where the money is

Most of a tournament's prize pool is paid at the final table, and every elimination moves everyone up one place. That makes ICM the dominant consideration in nearly every decision. How strong the effect is depends on the shape of the payouts.

Take six players with stacks of 40, 25, 15, 10, 6 and 4 (in big blinds or thousands, it does not matter for ICM).

Notice the flat column. When places pay nearly the same, extra chips are worth very little, since even first prize is not much more than sixth, but busting still costs a whole place. So the cost of losing dwarfs the gain from winning, and callers must be extremely tight. Steep payouts reward accumulating chips, which keeps bubble factors lower.

Three more patterns come out of tables like this one.

**The leader's shoves are hard to call.** A medium stack calling the leader risks its tournament for a double-up worth much less. Big stacks should open and shove wide, especially into medium stacks.

**Medium stacks should avoid each other.** When two of them clash, one busts, and everyone not in the hand moves up a place for free. Those spots have high bubble factors on both sides.

**The leader calls more freely.** Losing hurts them, but it does not end their tournament.

## Short stacks

A very short stack is tempted to fold and wait for others to bust. This **laddering** works only when another stack is shorter and about to be forced in by the blinds. Otherwise waiting costs the blinds every orbit and shrinks the fold equity of a later shove. With little to lose, the shortest stack has the lowest bubble factor at the table and should take good spots: shoving into players who cannot afford to call.

## Deals

Final tables sometimes agree to split the remaining prize money. The two common methods are a **chip-chop**, which pays in proportion to chips and favors the leader, and an **ICM chop**, which pays each player their ICM equity. The table at the top of this lesson is an ICM chop. Knowing your number before negotiating is the main defense against a bad deal. ICM ignores skill, so a much stronger player may reasonably ask for more.

<Remember>

- Flat payouts make survival nearly everything and push bubble factors up. Steep payouts reward chips
- Big stacks shove and open wide. Others must call tightly against them
- Medium stacks avoid clashes with each other
- Short stacks take risk unless a shorter stack is about to bust
- An ICM chop pays each player their ICM equity

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Calling the chip leader's shove with a medium stack and a hand that is only a chip-EV call
- Playing big pots between medium stacks
- Folding into the blinds as the short stack with no one shorter
- Agreeing to a chip-chop as a short stack

</Mistakes>
