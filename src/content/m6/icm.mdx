<Plain>Tournament chips are not money. Doubling your chips does not double your prize, but losing them all ends your tournament. ICM turns chip stacks into how much money each player is really worth.</Plain>

## Turning chips into money

At any moment in a tournament, each player has some expected share of the prize pool. That share is their **equity**, in money. The **Independent Chip Model** (ICM) estimates it from stacks and payouts alone.

The model makes one assumption and applies it repeatedly.

1. A player's chance of finishing **first** equals their share of the chips.
2. Given who finished first, the chance of each remaining player finishing **second** is their share of the chips that remain.
3. And so on down the places.

Each player's equity is the sum over places of the probability of finishing there times what that place pays.

## Why it compresses

The short stack is guaranteed 20 for being in the room. Every paid place pays something, so just surviving while someone else busts is worth money. The big stack can never win more than first prize, 50, however many chips it collects.

That gives the central asymmetry of tournament poker. If A's stack goes from 5,000 to 10,000, equity rises from {num(e[0], 1)} to {num(doubled[0], 1)}. If A loses the 5,000, equity falls from {num(e[0], 1)} to {num(busted[0], 1)}. Chips won are worth less than chips lost. Every risky decision is more expensive than its chip arithmetic suggests.

## What the model leaves out

ICM is a model of chip value and nothing else.

- **Skill.** A better player's chips are worth more than a weaker player's.
- **Position and blinds.** A short stack about to pay the big blind is worse off than one with a full orbit to wait.
- **Future play.** Big stacks can apply pressure on the bubble, which is worth money ICM does not count.

Even so, it is the standard tool, and its main prediction, that risk costs more than reward pays near the money, is robust. The next lesson turns it into a calling rule.

<Remember>

- ICM: chance of first = chip share, then the same rule among the remaining players for each lower place
- Equity = sum of P(place) × payout
- Short stacks are worth more than their chip share, big stacks less
- Chips won are worth less than chips lost
- With winner-take-all, ICM equity is chip share
- ICM ignores skill, position and the blinds

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Treating tournament chips as cash near the money
- Expecting a doubled stack to double equity
- Forgetting the model ignores skill and position
- Using ICM where the payouts are winner-take-all

</Mistakes>
