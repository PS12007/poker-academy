## Money on each head

In a **bounty** tournament (also called knockout), part of each entry goes to a bounty on that player. Knock them out and you collect it at once. In **progressive** bounty formats, half goes into your pocket and half onto your own head, so bounties grow as players accumulate them.

Bounties change the reward for winning a hand against a player you cover. That reward is extra pot, and pot odds can include it.

## Bounties in pot odds

Convert the bounty to chips. A simple rate: the starting stack is worth the part of the entry that goes into the main prize pool. If the entry is split evenly between pool and bounty, a starting stack of 10,000 chips buys 50 of prize money, so each 1 of bounty is worth 200 chips. Late in a tournament, when chips are worth less per chip, the conversion favors bounties further.

Then treat the bounty as part of the pot.

<Formula>equity needed = call / (pot + bounty in chips + call)</Formula>

## Who can collect

You can win a bounty only from a player you **cover**: you have at least as many chips as they do. If they cover you, winning the pot leaves them alive. Two practical effects:

- Big stacks have bounties available against everyone. Short stacks have few.
- When you are covered, play as if there were no bounty. When you cover, call wider.

## Adjusting ranges

- **Calling short stacks:** much wider, because the bounty adds directly to the pot.
- **Shoving as a short stack:** expect more calls, since others are calling for your bounty. Shove tighter and lean toward hands that do well when called.
- **Big bounties:** in progressive formats, players with large bounties attract calls and should tighten their shoves.
- **Postflop:** when a player is all-in and others remain, everyone with the cover has a reason to continue. Isolating the short stack becomes more valuable.

## Less ICM

Part of the prize pool is paid for knockouts rather than finishing places, so the payout ladder is smaller and bubble factors are lower. Bounty tournaments play looser through the bubble than standard ones, and chip-EV ranges are a better starting point.

<Remember>

- Convert the bounty to chips and add it to the pot
- You can only win a bounty from a player you cover
- Call short stacks much wider. Shove a little tighter as a short stack
- Bounties shrink the payout ladder, so ICM pressure is lower

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Ignoring bounties in pot odds
- Calling for a bounty you cannot win because the player covers you
- Shoving a standard range as a short stack when everyone is hunting your bounty
- Applying full ICM tightening in a bounty event

</Mistakes>
