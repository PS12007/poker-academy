## Raise first in

When everyone before you has folded, you are **first in**, and the right play with any hand worth entering the pot is a raise. Module 1 gave this as a rule. Here is the arithmetic behind it.

There is 1.5bb in the pot from the blinds. If you raise to 2.5bb and everyone folds, you win 1.5bb at once. Treat the raise as a pure bluff for a moment: it risks 2.5 to win 1.5, so it breaks even if it works {pct(steal)} of the time. From the button only two players can call, and each folds well over half their hands to a raise.

That is before counting what your cards are worth. When you are called you still have a hand, often the better one, and usually position. So the bar for opening is much lower than {pct(steal, 0)}, and a wide range of hands clears it from late position.

Calling the big blind does none of this. It cannot win the blinds, it invites everyone in cheaply, and it tells the table your hand is not strong.

## Ranges by seat

Each seat has its own opening range. Earlier seats face more players and worse position, so they play fewer hands.

Notice how the ranges grow. Every seat keeps all the hands of the seat before it and adds the next tier: more suited aces, more suited connectors, then offsuit broadway hands, and on the button a long tail of suited hands and offsuit aces.

The small blind is a special case. Only one player is left, which argues for a wide range, but you will be out of position for the whole hand, which argues against. The chart resolves it with a range a little wider than the cutoff's.

## How big

A raise to between **2 and 3 big blinds** is standard, and 2.5bb is a good default. The size is a trade.

- Larger raises win more when the blinds fold and charge callers more, but risk more when you are re-raised and must fold.
- Smaller raises risk less, which lets you open more hands, but give the big blind a better price to defend.

At 3bb a pure steal needs to work {pct(steal3, 0)} of the time, against {pct(steal, 0)} at 2.5bb.

**Add one big blind per limper.** Players who have already called make the pot bigger and are getting a better price. With two limpers, open to about 4.5 or 5bb.

**Use one size for the whole range.** If aces get a big raise and suited connectors a small one, anyone paying attention knows which you have.

## Adjusting

The charts are a baseline for unknown opponents. Shift them when you know something.

- Blinds that fold too much: open wider, especially on the button.
- A player behind who re-raises often: open tighter and prefer hands that can continue.
- Calling stations in the blinds: drop the weakest suited hands and add high cards that make top pair.

<Remember>

- First in, raise or fold. Do not limp
- Default open is 2.5bb, plus one big blind per limper
- Earlier seat, tighter range. Each seat adds a tier to the one before
- A 2.5bb steal breaks even at 62.5% folds with a worthless hand, and real hands need far less
- One size for every hand in the range

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Limping in behind nobody
- Opening the same range from every seat
- Sizing up with strong hands and down with weak ones
- Forgetting to size up over limpers

</Mistakes>
