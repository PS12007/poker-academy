## The discount

The big blind has already paid one blind. Facing an open to 2.5bb with the small blind folded, the pot holds 4bb and calling costs only 1.5bb more.

  { label: 'Cost to call', expr: '2.5 − 1', result: '1.5bb' },
  { label: 'Equity needed', expr: '1.5 / (4 + 1.5)', result: pct(price(2.5)) },

Almost any two cards have {pct(price(2.5), 0)} raw equity against a button opening range. Seven-two offsuit comes close. If pot odds were the whole story, the big blind would never fold.

## Why you still fold

Raw equity assumes the hand is checked to showdown. The big blind is out of position against a player with the betting lead, and it will be bet off many hands that had equity. This is the equity realization effect from Module 2 at full strength.

A rough working rule: an offsuit, unconnected hand out of position captures perhaps two thirds of its raw equity, while a suited connected hand captures close to all of it. So the hands that defend are not chosen by raw equity alone. Suited hands, connected hands and hands with an ace or king defend. Offsuit junk folds, even when its raw equity clears the price.

The price still matters. It is the reason the big blind's range is far wider than any other seat's response to a raise.

## The small blind

The small blind is the hardest seat. It has paid half as much, so its price is worse. It is first to act on every later street. And the big blind is still behind, able to re-raise.

For those reasons most strong players use a **3-bet or fold** strategy from the small blind against a raise, with a linear range: the best hands 3-bet, the rest fold, and very little calls.

When everyone folds to the small blind, it is a contest against one player. Raising a wide range is standard, as in the opening chart.

## Adjustments

- **Smaller opens, wider defense.** Against a minimum raise, defend most suited hands and many offsuit ones.
- **Larger opens, tighter defense.** Against 3bb or more, drop the weakest hands first.
- **Multiway.** If another player has already called, your price improves but your equity share falls. Favor hands that make strong draws and fold dominated offsuit hands.
- **Rake.** In raked games, marginal defends lose their small edge. Tighten a little, and 3-bet some hands instead of calling since pots won before the flop are often unraked.
