<Plain>You never know an opponent's exact cards, so think in terms of all the hands they could have. A quick shorthand like QQ+ or AKs lets you write those groups down and count them.</Plain>

## Why think in ranges

You never know an opponent's two cards. You know what they did, and each action is consistent with a set of hands. That set is their **range**. Strong players do not guess a single hand and hope. They carry a range, cut it down as the hand goes on, and act against all of it at once.

Your own play is a range as well. In any spot you would take the same action with many hands, and your opponents are playing against that whole set.

So a working player needs a fast way to write ranges down and count them.

## The grid

The 13 by 13 grid holds all 169 starting hands.

- The **diagonal** is pocket pairs, aces at the top left.
- **Above** the diagonal are suited hands.
- **Below** are offsuit hands.

The grid is not to scale. Each cell looks the same size, but a pair is 6 combos, a suited hand 4 and an offsuit hand 12. The offsuit half of the grid holds 936 of the 1,326 combos. A range that looks small on the grid can be large if it sits below the diagonal.

## From hands to a percentage

A range is often quoted as a percentage of all hands, which is its combos divided by 1,326.

- The range {ex} is {c(ex)} combos, {pct(rangeFraction(parseRange(ex)))} of hands.
- The range {wide} is {c(wide)} combos, {pct(rangeFraction(parseRange(wide)))}.

Percentages are for communicating size. For decisions, count the combos that matter after removing the cards you can see. On a board of A-K-4, the tight range above drops from {c(ex)} combos to {c(ex, 'AsKd4c')}.

## Weights

Good strategies sometimes play one hand two ways. A hand might 3-bet half the time and call the other half. In a range that is written as a weight between 0 and 1, and the hand contributes that share of its combos.

You do not need mixed frequencies to play well at this stage. Weights are most useful for describing an opponent you are unsure about: a player who probably has ace-queen here but not always.

<Remember>

- Pairs on the diagonal, suited above, offsuit below
- Pair 6 combos, suited 4, offsuit 12. The grid is not to scale

- Percentage of hands = combos / 1,326
- A weight is a frequency. Count combos after removing visible cards

<Mistakes>

- Putting an opponent on one exact hand
- Judging the size of a range by how much of the grid it covers
- Forgetting your own range is visible to observant opponents
- Counting combos without removing the board and your hand

</Mistakes>
