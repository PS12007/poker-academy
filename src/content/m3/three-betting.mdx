## Two reasons to re-raise

A **3-bet** is a re-raise over an open. Like every bet it has to be for value or a bluff.

**For value.** Your hand is ahead of the range that will continue against it. Aces and kings always qualify. Whether queens, jacks and ace-king do depends on who opened and from where.

**As a bluff.** Your hand is not ahead of their continuing range, but they will fold often enough, and the hand has something going for it when they do not.

The second reason is what makes the first one pay. A player who only 3-bets aces and kings gets no action, because everyone folds. Adding bluffs forces opponents to continue with worse hands or surrender the pot.

## Polarized ranges

Facing a raise in position, you have three choices with any hand: 3-bet, call or fold. That lets you sort hands by what they want.

- **The strongest hands** want to build the pot: 3-bet for value.
- **Good hands that play well after the flop** (medium pairs, suited broadway) prefer to call. They are ahead of the opener's range but behind the range that continues against a 3-bet.
- **Hands just short of a call** make the best bluffs. Folding them costs little, and they have blockers or playability.
- **Everything else** folds.

A range of best hands plus bluffs, with the middle calling, is **polarized**.

## Linear ranges

Sometimes calling is a poor option. From the small blind you would be out of position with the big blind still to act. Against a player who never folds to 3-bets, bluffs burn money.

In those spots use a **linear** range: 3-bet the best hands you would play, in order of strength, and fold the rest. No bluffs, and few or no calls. A linear range against a loose opener might be tens and better, ace-queen and better, and the best suited broadways.

## Choosing bluffs

Two properties make a good 3-bet bluff.

1. **Blockers.** An ace in your hand removes half the combos of pocket aces and a quarter of ace-king from their range. Those are the hands that 4-bet you.
2. **Playability when called.** Suited aces make nut flushes and wheel straights. Suited connectors flop draws that can keep betting.

That is why A-5 suited through A-2 suited appear in almost every bluffing range. An offsuit hand like K-7 has neither property: it blocks little and flops badly.

## Sizing

- **In position:** about 3 times the open. Over 2.5bb, make it 7.5 to 8bb.
- **Out of position:** about 4 times. Over 2.5bb, make it 10 to 11bb.

Out of position you want more folds and a smaller stack-to-pot ratio if called, so you charge more.

As a pure bluff, 8bb over a 2.5bb open risks 8 to win 4 and needs {pct(need)} folds. Few opponents fold that often, which is the point of choosing bluffs with equity: the hand wins a share of the pots where it gets called.

<Remember>

- 3-bet for value when ahead of the range that continues, or as a bluff with blockers and playability
- Polarized: best hands and bluffs 3-bet, the middle calls
- Linear: best hands only, in order. Use it when calling is bad or folds are rare
- Size about 3 times the open in position, 4 times out of position
- Against tight openers 3-bet less. Against loose late openers 3-bet more

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- 3-betting only aces and kings, so nobody pays you
- Turning good calling hands like K-Q suited into 3-bets that only better hands continue against
- Bluffing with offsuit junk that has no blockers
- Bluffing players who never fold before the flop

</Mistakes>
