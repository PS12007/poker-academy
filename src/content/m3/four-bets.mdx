## The fourth and fifth bets

Each re-raise narrows the ranges. By the time someone 4-bets, they hold a very strong hand or a bluff chosen with care, and almost nothing between.

**4-bet sizing.** At 100bb, a 4-bet is usually **2.2 to 2.5 times the 3-bet**: 20 to 24bb over a 3-bet to 9. That looks small, but the pot is already big and a larger size would commit you. A small 4-bet keeps the option of folding your bluffs if they shove.

**4-bet ranges.** Value is aces and kings always, with queens and ace-king joining against opponents who 3-bet wide. Bluffs are few and nearly always hold an ace or a king as a blocker: A-5 suited is the standard example again.

**The 5-bet** at 100bb is all-in. There is no room for a sixth bet.

## Every raise shrinks the SPR

The **stack-to-pot ratio** (SPR) is the effective stack divided by the pot at the start of the flop. It measures how much play is left.

| Pot type | Pot on the flop | Stack behind | SPR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single raised | 5.5bb | 97.5bb | {num(sprSrp, 1)} |
| 3-bet | 17.5bb | 92bb | {num(spr3, 1)} |
| 4-bet | 45.5bb | 78bb | {num(spr4, 1)} |

At an SPR near {num(sprSrp, 0)}, stacks go in over three large bets and only very strong hands want that. At {num(spr3, 0)}, two bets do it and an overpair is usually happy. At {num(spr4, 1)}, one bet is all-in, and any pair or good draw is committed. Module 4 has a lesson on planning around this number.

## Stack depth changes hand values

The 100bb charts are not universal.

**Shorter stacks (20 to 40bb)** remove postflop play. Hands are valued by all-in equity, because that is how they will be played. High cards and pairs go up. Suited connectors and small suited aces go down, since there is no money behind to win when they hit. 3-bets become shoves or are sized to commit.

**Deeper stacks (150bb and more)** do the opposite. Implied odds grow, so hands that make the nuts go up. One-pair hands go down, because the pots they win are small and the pots they lose are huge. Even kings can fold before the flop at extreme depth.

<Remember>

- 4-bet to about 2.2 to 2.5 times the 3-bet. At 100bb the 5-bet is all-in
- 4-bet bluffs hold an ace or king blocker
- Shove EV = fold% × pot + (1 − fold%) × (equity × final pot − risk)
- SPR falls from about 18 to 5 to under 2 as the pot is raised, 3-bet and 4-bet
- Short stacks reward raw equity. Deep stacks reward nut potential

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- 4-betting so large that you cannot fold a bluff
- Stacking off with queens or ace-king against a player who only 4-bets aces and kings
- Using 100bb ranges with 30bb or 250bb
- Ignoring that a 3-bet pot plays for stacks far more easily

</Mistakes>
