## One card changes everything

On the flop there are two cards to come and equities are fluid. The turn removes half of that. Made hands gain, because draws now have one chance instead of two. Draws lose, unless the turn card was theirs.

## Good and bad turn cards

Think in ranges again. A turn card is good for you if it improves your range more than your opponent's.

**Good for the preflop raiser:**

- Overcards to the flop, especially aces and kings. The raiser holds more of them, and they demote the caller's flopped pairs.
- Cards that leave the board dry.

**Good for the caller:**

- Cards that complete flush draws and straight draws. The caller's range has more of those hands after calling a flop bet.
- Low cards that pair the board, when the caller has more of that rank.
- Middle cards that connect with the flop.

**Neutral:** low cards that change nothing, often called bricks. A brick favors whoever was ahead.

## The second barrel

After betting the flop and being called, you choose again. The caller's range is now stronger than it was: the weakest hands folded.

**Barrel for value** with hands that are still ahead of the calling range. Top pair with a good kicker usually qualifies on a safe turn.

**Barrel as a bluff** when at least one of these holds:

- The turn card favors your range and scares theirs.
- You picked up equity: a flush draw, a straight draw, or an overcard.
- You hold blockers to their strongest hands.

**Give up** with hands that have no equity and no good card. A bluff that had fold equity on the flop loses much of it once the opponent has shown they have something.

**Check with the middle.** Medium hands such as second pair, or top pair with a weak kicker, are too weak to bet twice for value and too strong to turn into bluffs.

## The pot is bigger now

A typical single raised pot is about 5.5bb on the flop. A half-pot bet and call makes it 11bb on the turn, and the same again makes it 22bb on the river. Each street roughly doubles the stakes. A mistake on the turn costs about twice what the same mistake costs on the flop, and river mistakes cost four times as much.

This is also why draws go down in value on the turn. The price has gone up and the chance of hitting has gone down. A flush draw that was a comfortable flop call is often a fold to a large turn bet, unless the implied odds are real.

<Remember>

- The turn cuts a draw's chances roughly in half and firms up made hands
- A turn card is good for you if it helps your range more than theirs
- Overcards favor the raiser. Completed draws and connected low cards favor the caller
- Barrel with value, or with bluffs that gained equity or a good card. Give up the rest
- Each street roughly doubles the pot, so later mistakes cost more

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Betting the turn automatically because you bet the flop
- Continuing a bluff on a card that completes the caller's draws
- Calling large turn bets with draws on flop-style pot odds
- Betting medium hands twice and facing a raise

</Mistakes>
