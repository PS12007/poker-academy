## Why the raiser bets

A **continuation bet**, or c-bet, is a flop bet from the player who raised last before the flop. It works for two reasons you already know.

First, most hands miss most flops. An unpaired hand flops a pair or better about a third of the time, and that is true for the caller too.

Second, the raiser usually has the stronger range. Betting makes the caller's many weak hands either fold their equity or pay.

The price is low. A bet of one third of the pot, treated as a pure bluff, needs to work only {pct(alpha(3, 1), 0)} of the time. The caller, to stop that bluff from profiting, must continue with {pct(mdf(3, 1), 0)} of their range, and on a board that missed them they often cannot.

## Two strategies

Solvers and strong players settle into one of two patterns on most flops.

**Bet small with most of the range.** Used when you have a clear range advantage and the board is **static**, meaning later cards rarely change who is ahead. Examples: A-K-4, K-7-2, Q-Q-5. A quarter to a third of the pot, with nearly everything: strong hands, weak pairs, overcards, air. The small size is enough because the opponent's range is weak and there is little to protect against.

**Bet large with part of the range, check the rest.** Used when the board is **dynamic**, meaning many turn cards complete draws or shift the lead, or when the advantage is small. Examples: J-T-8 two-tone, 9-8-6 two-tone. Two thirds of the pot or more, with a polarized set of hands: strong made hands that need to charge draws, and draws that can win by folding the opponent or by improving. Medium hands check, because a big bet with them folds out worse and gets called by better.

**Mostly check** on the boards from the last lesson where the caller holds the nut advantage: 7-6-5, 9-8-7, 6-5-4.

At a third of the pot, the caller needs only {pct(potOdds(4, 1), 0)} equity and should defend about three hands in four. That is why a small bet targets the whole range: it asks even ace-high and bottom pair for a decision. At the full pot the caller can fold half their range, so a large bet only makes sense when you want strong second-best hands to pay and weak ones are welcome to leave.

## Which hands to bet

For a small bet with the whole range, there is nothing to choose. For a larger, selective bet:

- **Value:** hands that can bet three streets and stay ahead of what calls. Top pair with a good kicker and better.
- **Bluffs:** hands with equity that have little showdown value. Flush draws, open-ended straight draws, gutshots with overcards, backdoor draws to the nuts.
- **Checks:** medium made hands such as second pair and weak top pair, which want to reach showdown cheaply, plus a few strong hands to protect the checking range.

## Position and number of players

**Out of position**, bet less often. You cannot close the action, a call gives the opponent position for two more streets, and checking keeps the pot smaller with your marginal hands.

**Multiway**, bet much less often and mostly for value. Against two opponents, a bluff needs both to fold. Check your misses and bet your strong hands larger.

<Remember>

- The raiser c-bets because ranges miss often and the raiser's range is stronger
- Static board with range advantage: bet small with almost everything
- Dynamic board: bet large with strong hands and draws, check the middle
- Caller has the nut advantage: mostly check
- Bet less out of position and far less multiway

<Mistakes>

- Betting every flop at the same size regardless of texture
- Betting big with medium hands
- Firing into three or four opponents with nothing
- Never checking a strong hand, so that your checks are always weak

</Mistakes>
