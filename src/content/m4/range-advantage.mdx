## Boards meet ranges, not hands

Before the flop you saw that each player holds a range. The flop does not treat ranges equally. Some boards connect with the hands the raiser tends to hold, and others with the caller's.

The running example for this module is the most common postflop spot: the **button opens and the big blind calls**. The button holds the teaching open range. The big blind holds the calling range from the blind defense lesson, which has no aces, kings or ace-king because those 3-bet.

Two measures describe how a flop treats the two ranges.

**Range advantage** is about the average. Whose range has more equity against the other's?

**Nut advantage** is about the top. Whose range contains a larger share of the hands strong enough to play for stacks?

**A-K-4.** The button has both advantages. It holds all the strong aces and kings, and the sets of aces and kings. The big blind's range is capped: most of its best pairs on this board are weak aces.

**K-7-2.** The button is ahead on average again, but the top of the ranges is close. Both players have sets of sevens and twos, and neither has many.

**7-6-5 and 9-8-7.** The equities are roughly level, and the big blind now holds more of the strong hands. Low connected cards are what a calling range is made of: small pairs for sets, suited connectors for straights and two pair. The button has these too, but they are a smaller part of a range full of high cards.

## What each advantage is for

**Range advantage supports betting often.** If your range is ahead on average, betting many hands for a small size makes the weaker range pay with all its marginal holdings. On A-K-4 the button can bet nearly everything small.

**Nut advantage supports betting big.** A large bet says you can have hands that want all the money in. If your opponent has more of those than you do, big bets get raised. On 9-8-7 the button should check often and size down, because the big blind can check-raise with a range full of straights, sets and two pair.

**When you have neither**, play defensively: check more, call rather than raise, and let the favored player bluff into you with their excess of weak hands.

<Remember>

- Range advantage: more equity on average. Nut advantage: more of the strongest hands
- The preflop raiser is favored on high-card boards. The caller catches up on low connected boards
- A caller's range is capped: its best hands would have re-raised
- Range advantage lets you bet often. Nut advantage lets you bet big
- With neither, check and defend

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Thinking only about your own two cards on the flop
- Betting big on boards where the caller holds more of the strong hands
- Assuming the preflop raiser is always the favorite
- Confusing average equity with who can have the nuts

</Mistakes>
