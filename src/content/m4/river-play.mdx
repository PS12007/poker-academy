<Plain>On the river there are no more cards, so only two reasons to bet remain: to get called by worse hands, or to make better hands fold. If neither will happen, check.</Plain>

## No more cards

The river is the simplest street in one way: nothing can change. Every hand either wins or loses at showdown. Draws are now busted or made. That removes two reasons for betting that applied earlier, protection and building a pot for a draw, and leaves only two.

- **Value:** worse hands call.
- **Bluff:** better hands fold.

A river bet that does neither is a mistake, and on the river it is easy to check which one you are making.

## Thin value

A value bet is profitable when you are ahead of **the range that calls**, not the range you face before betting. That difference is where most missed value hides.

Suppose you hold top pair with a decent kicker and your opponent checks to you. Their range holds some better hands, many worse pairs, and busted draws. If you bet half the pot, the busted draws fold. Of the hands that call, how many do you beat? If the answer is more than half, bet.

Betting hands that are only modestly ahead of the calling range is called **thin value**. Winning players do it constantly. Typical opponents call too much on the river, so a hand that feels marginal is often well ahead of what pays it off.

Two cautions. In position, the bet closes the action unless you are raised. Out of position, a check may induce a bluff from worse hands that would have folded to a bet, so some medium hands earn more by checking and calling. And if a raise is likely, you need a bigger margin, since you will often have to fold the best hand or pay off a better one.

## Bluff catching

Now the other side. Your opponent bets the river and you hold a hand that loses to everything they bet for value. Its only way to win is if they are bluffing. Such a hand is a **bluff catcher**.

With no cards to come, your equity is the fraction of their betting range that is bluffs. Compare it with the price.

In practice, most players at low and middle stakes bluff the river less than the price requires, especially with large bets and raises. Against an unknown opponent, folding bluff catchers to big river bets and to river raises is a sound default. Against players known to bluff, the same hand is a call.

## Polarized and merged

A river betting range has one of two shapes.

**Polarized.** Very strong hands and bluffs, nothing between. The medium hands check, because they beat bluffs anyway and lose to value. Polarized ranges use large sizes: the strong hands want maximum payment and the bluffs want maximum folds. For the caller to be indifferent, the share of bluffs must equal the caller's pot odds. At a pot-sized bet that is {pct(bluffFraction(1, 1), 0)} bluffs. At half pot, {pct(bluffFraction(2, 1), 0)}.

**Merged.** Strong and medium hands together, few bluffs, smaller sizes. This is the thin-value shape. It works against opponents who call too wide, because your medium hands get paid by even weaker ones.

Which to use depends on the opponent's range. Against a capped range full of bluff catchers, polarize and bet big. Against a wide, sticky range, merge and bet smaller.

## Choosing bluffs

On the river a bluff needs fold equity alone. A pot-sized bluff must work {pct(alpha(1, 1), 0)} of the time and a half-pot bluff {pct(alpha(2, 1), 0)}.

Pick hands by two rules.

1. **No showdown value.** If a hand might win by checking, checking is better. Busted draws are the natural bluffs.
2. **Good blockers.** Prefer hands that hold cards your opponent needs for a call, and that do not hold the cards they would fold. A later lesson covers this in detail.

Keep the number of bluffs in proportion. Too few and nobody needs to pay your value bets. Too many and every bluff catcher is a call.

<Remember>

- On the river, bet only for value or as a bluff
- Value bet when you beat more than half of the hands that call
- A bluff catcher's equity is the opponent's bluff frequency. Compare it with pot odds
- Polarized ranges bet big with nuts and air. Merged ranges bet smaller with strong and medium hands
- Bluff with hands that cannot win a showdown
- Big river bets and raises from typical players are rarely bluffs

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Checking back hands that would be called by worse
- Bluffing with a hand that would have won at showdown
- Calling large river bets because your hand looks strong in absolute terms
- Betting a medium hand that only better hands call

</Mistakes>
