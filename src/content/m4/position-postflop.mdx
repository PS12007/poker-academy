<Plain>Acting last is a big advantage: you see what your opponent does before you decide. When you act first, check most of the time and sometimes check-raise your best hands and best draws.</Plain>

## What last action buys

Module 1 said position is information. After the flop it is worth three specific things.

1. **You see their action first.** A check from them removes many strong hands from their range before you decide.
2. **You choose whether the round ends.** Checking behind guarantees a free card. Out of position, a check only offers one.
3. **You control the pot.** With a medium hand you can check once and keep the pot small. With a strong hand you can bet every street.

The out-of-position player has none of these. Their check invites a bet, their bet can be raised, and whatever they do, they must do it again on the next street without knowing more.

## Playing out of position

The player acting first is not helpless. The plan has three parts.

**Check most of your range to the raiser.** If you called before the flop, the raiser has the lead and usually the stronger range. Betting into them, called a donk bet, is right only on boards that shift the nut advantage to you, and even then rarely.

**Defend enough.** Against a bet of a third of the pot, a pure bluff needs to work {pct(alpha(3, 1), 0)} of the time, so you must continue with about {pct(mdf(3, 1), 0)} of your range or their bluffs print money. Against a pot-sized bet the figure is {pct(mdf(1, 1), 0)}. These are guides: on boards that favor the bettor you can fold a little more, and on boards that favor you, less.

**Check-raise.** Calling alone lets the bettor realize all their equity cheaply. Raising some of the time punishes automatic bets and builds pots with your best hands.

## The check-raise

A check-raising range is polarized, like a 3-bet.

- **Value:** two pair, sets and straights, the hands that want a big pot at once. On 7-6-5 against a button bet, the big blind's calling range holds two pair or better {pct(share(BB, '7s6s5d', 'twoPair'))} of the time against the button's {pct(share(BTN, '7s6s5d', 'twoPair'))}. That is a board to check-raise often.
- **Bluffs:** draws. Open-ended straight draws, flush draws, and gutshots with a backdoor flush draw. They win immediately when the bettor folds and have outs when called.

Medium hands call. Top pair with an ordinary kicker gains nothing by raising: worse hands fold and better hands continue.

Size the raise to about three times the bet in position and a little more out of position. On wet boards go larger, because draws are getting a price.

## Protecting your checks

If you bet all your strong hands and check all your weak ones, your opponent knows what a check means and bets every time. The fix is to put some strong hands in the checking range. This matters on both sides of the table.

- **Out of position as the caller:** your check-raises with strong hands do this job.
- **Out of position as the raiser:** check some overpairs and top pairs, planning to call.
- **In position:** check back some medium hands that are happy to see a free card, and a few strong ones on dry boards where slow play is safe.

## Using position with a medium hand

Say you hold second pair in position and the opponent checks. Betting three streets is too much: by the river only better hands are calling. Checking the flop or turn once gets you to showdown at a price the hand can afford, and it also induces bluffs on later streets from hands you beat. This is **pot control**, and only the player in position can do it reliably.

<Remember>

- Position means seeing their action, closing the betting, and controlling the pot
- Out of position, mostly check to the raiser, then call or check-raise
- MDF against a third-pot bet is 75%. Against a pot bet, 50%
- Check-raise the strongest hands and draws. Call with the middle
- Keep strong hands in your checking range

</Remember>

<Mistakes>

- Folding too much to small bets
- Check-raising top pair and folding to the re-raise
- Betting out of position with medium hands that would rather see a cheap showdown
- Checking only when weak

</Mistakes>
